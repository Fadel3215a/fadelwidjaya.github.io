Closing MSA Documentation to Enable Stage 3 Progression

How I supported the coordination and document control of Material Sample Approval (MSA) documentation on the LRT Jakarta project — moving unresolved documents from NONO-C status through approval closure to Stage 3 progression.

Origin

During the LRT Jakarta project, I supported the coordination and document control of Material Sample Approval (MSA) documentation. Some MSA documents remained in NONO-C status because comments and required approval documentation had not yet been fully closed.

MSA closure required an approval page and supporting checklist/documentation. Relevant parties needed to review and sign the required documentation before the MSA could become fully closed and proceed to the next project stage.

Problem

An MSA could not progress to Stage 3 until the outstanding comments and required approval documentation were completed. The process involved multiple parties, including engineering personnel, the contractor, PMC, and other relevant project stakeholders.

From a coordination perspective, the blocker was not a single missing signature — it was a chain of unresolved dependencies across roles that each held part of the closure requirement.

Investigation / information gathering

I reviewed the outstanding requirements and the MSA documentation checklist to understand what was still required for closure. I then discussed the issue with the Engineer, Chief Engineer, and Site Engineer Manager to confirm the appropriate next steps before escalating the matter externally.

This internal verification step was important: escalating without confirming requirements risked requesting the wrong action or involving the wrong parties.

Coordination & action

After confirming the requirements internally, I prepared a follow-up/warning letter to the PMC requesting action on the outstanding comments. I then coordinated the required approval process and followed up with the relevant parties to obtain the necessary signatures and supporting documentation.

My role throughout was coordination, document control, follow-up, and communication — supporting the closure process rather than making engineering or approval decisions independently.

Process overview

Generic workflow based on the MSA closure process. Does not reproduce project documents, signatures, or identifying information.

  1. Status NONO-C
  2. Blocker Outstanding comments / requirements
  3. Internal review Engineering review & requirement confirmation
  4. External follow-up PMC follow-up on outstanding comments
  5. Approvals Required approvals & signatures
  6. Status NONO (fully closed)
  7. Progression Stage 3
  8. Result Final documentation

Outcome

The outstanding MSA requirements were addressed, allowing the documents to move from NONO-C to fully closed NONO status and proceed to Stage 3, where the documentation could be finalized.

What I learned

This experience taught me that document control is closely connected to project progression. A document can appear administratively complete while still containing unresolved dependencies that prevent the next stage from moving forward.

I also learned the importance of verifying an issue internally before escalating it externally, involving the correct stakeholders, and maintaining clear follow-up until the required action is completed. Coordination work is often less about making decisions and more about ensuring the right information reaches the right people at the right time — and that nothing is marked closed before it actually is.
