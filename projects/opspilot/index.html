AI · Operations Audit

OpsPilot — AI Native Operations Audit

An AI Operations Audit platform built to explore how AI-native tooling can support structured operational analysis.

View live project

Origin

OpsPilot came from my interest in combining operations knowledge with AI-assisted workflows. Through my work in infrastructure project coordination and my exploration of AI development tools, I wanted to understand whether AI could support operational analysis in a more structured way — not as a generic chat interface, but as a platform designed around how operational problems are identified and reviewed.

Problem

Organizations can have operational inefficiencies that are difficult to identify systematically. Review and audit work often happens in ad-hoc documents, disconnected conversations, or informal observations — without a structured environment for analyzing how operations actually function.

I was interested in whether an AI-native platform could help structure that analysis — giving operational review a dedicated workflow rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Approach

I explored how an AI-native system could structure operational analysis and audit workflows. This meant thinking about what an operations audit actually involves — identifying areas to review, organizing findings, and iterating on analysis — and designing a platform around those steps rather than around AI capabilities alone.

I used Cursor IDE as the primary development environment, applying AI-assisted development to build and iterate on the platform itself. The development process was part of the learning: using AI tools to create a product focused on AI-enabled operational work.

Implementation

OpsPilot was developed as an AI Operations Audit platform — a web application designed to support structured operational analysis. I built the platform iteratively, refining the workflow and interface as I tested how operational audit concepts translated into a digital product.

The platform is deployed and accessible at ops-pilot-sand.vercel.app.

Outcome

The deliverable is a working AI Operations Audit platform — a functional web application that demonstrates how operational analysis can be structured within an AI-native environment. OpsPilot exists as a built and deployed product, available for review and exploration.

It is a personal project developed to explore the intersection of operations knowledge and AI-assisted tooling. It has not been validated through commercial deployment or documented business outcomes.

What I learned

  • AI-assisted development. Building a product with Cursor IDE showed me how AI tools can accelerate development while still requiring clear thinking about what to build and why.
  • Structuring operational problems for AI systems. AI platforms need operational context defined clearly — vague inputs produce vague analysis, regardless of the underlying model.
  • Product thinking. Moving from "AI can analyze operations" to an actual platform required decisions about workflow, scope, and what the user experience should feel like.
  • Translating operational knowledge into a digital product. My coordination and reporting experience informed how I thought about audit workflows, even though OpsPilot is a separate project from my infrastructure work.
  • Iterating on an AI-enabled workflow. Early versions revealed gaps in how analysis was structured. Each iteration refined both the platform and my understanding of what operational audit actually needs from a tool.
